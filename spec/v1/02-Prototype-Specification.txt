Prototype Specification
Two Cases as the Testing Ground for the Provision-Survival Pipeline
Andrew Vera · Kenyon College · IPHS 484 Senior Seminar
Version 1.0 — September 2026



0. Prerequisite
This document assumes the companion document, What Survives: Measuring Legislative Alteration of Voter-Approved Ballot Initiatives
— Project Context and Design, has been read. Terms used here without definition — spine ID, witness, the nine layers, exemption sweep
— are defined there.




1. What the prototype is for
The prototype codes two ballot initiatives end to end and compares them. It is not a scaled-down version of the project. It is an
instrument test, and it has four jobs:

Find out whether the schema survives contact with real statutory text. Nine layers of tags were designed in the abstract. Some
categories will not fit what legislatures actually write. Some values will go unused. Something will need a slot that does not exist. All of
this is cheaper to discover on two cases than on fifteen.

Establish the real cost per case. The decision to publish fifteen hand-verified cases is provisional and rests on a guess. Timing two
cases honestly — including the hand-coding, not just the pipeline run — produces the number that determines project scope.

Test whether the alignment step works on legal text. This is the technical risk. Semantic matching of provisions across
renumbering and rewording is the part that could simply fail, and the prototype is where that is found out.

Produce the first real comparison. Two cases coded with the same instrument, set side by side, is the smallest object that
demonstrates what the tool is for.

A prototype that reveals the schema is wrong has succeeded.




2. The two cases

2.1 Recommended pair
Missouri Proposition A (2024) and South Dakota Initiated Measure 22 (2016).


2.2 Case 1 — Missouri Proposition A

 Field                                                     Value

 Approved                                                  November 2024, ~58%

 Vehicle                                                   Initiated statute, direct

 Rule regime                                               Unrestricted

 Altering instrument                                       HB 567, signed July 10, 2025

 Effective                                                 August 28, 2025

 Profile                                                   Selective partial hollowing


Proposition A did three things: raised the minimum wage to $15, indexed future adjustments to the Consumer Price Index from 2027,
and required most private employers to provide earned paid sick time at one hour per thirty worked. The sick leave requirement took
effect May 1, 2025. HB 567 repealed the paid sick time provisions entirely and repealed the inflation indexing. The $15 wage survived.

What this case exercises:

    Alignment across partial survival. Some provisions survive, some vanish, some may be reworded. This is the real alignment
    problem, not a trivial version of it.
    The exemption sweep. The minimum wage provision survives textually. Without the sweep, it codes as intact . With it, the stripped
    indexing should surface. If the sweep does not catch this, the sweep does not work.
    Layer 2’s escalation/core-obligation distinction. This case is the reason that distinction exists. If the coding does not separate
    the $15 from the CPI indexing, Layer 2 has failed its design purpose.
    Layer 4 mechanisms — at minimum outright repeal ; possibly others.
    Layer 5 description gap. HB 567’s framing against its measured effect.
    Layer 8 escalation cycle. Advocates subsequently pursued the same policy as a constitutional amendment.
    Layer 6 before/after-effect field. Sick leave was repealed roughly four months after it began operating.


2.3 Case 2 — South Dakota Initiated Measure 22

 Field                                                                       Value

 Approved                                                                    November 2016

 Vehicle                                                                     Initiated statute

 Rule regime                                                                 Unrestricted

 Altering instrument                                                         HB 1069, introduced January 20, 2017; signed February 2, 2017

 Profile                                                                     Total repeal, ~2 months


Measure 22 was a campaign finance, lobbying and ethics package that limited political contributions, created a publicly funded
campaign finance program, and established a state ethics commission to enforce it. It was repealed outright roughly two months after
passage. Because HB 1069 carried an emergency clause, citizens were barred from mounting a veto referendum against the repeal.

What this case exercises:

    The degenerate case. Every provision is deleted. The aligner should return none for all of them. If it instead forces matches against
    unrelated surviving statutory text, the most dangerous failure mode in the pipeline has been caught on a real case rather than a
    synthetic one. This case functions as a live negative control.
    Layer 4’s referendum-proof clause attachment . The emergency clause is the reason that value exists in the schema.
    Layer 6 speed and procedural fields. Two months is the fast extreme.
    Cross-state portability. Two different state code structures, retrieval paths, and drafting conventions. A pipeline that only works on
    Missouri is not a pipeline.


2.4 Why this pair rather than another
Three reasons, in order:

Maximum contrast on the outcome variable. Total repeal against selective partial hollowing. If the tool cannot distinguish these two
cases clearly, it cannot distinguish anything, and that is worth knowing in week one.

One variable held constant. Both states are unrestricted regimes. The prototype is testing the instrument, not the doctrinal
hypothesis, so holding rule regime constant removes a source of variation that would complicate interpretation of pipeline behavior.

Cross-state from the start. Building on two states forces the retrieval and parsing layers to generalize immediately rather than being
quietly overfit to one state’s code structure.


2.5 Documented alternative
Missouri Proposition A + Missouri Proposition B (2010). Same state, fourteen years apart, both selective. Proposition B had its
fifty-dog cap, breeding frequency limit and cage-height requirement stripped while its care standards and criminal penalty survived —
which exercises threshold adjustment and numeric-provision handling better than either recommended case.

The trade-off: two selective cases give a richer test of Layer 4, but lose the degenerate-case check and the cross-state portability test.
This pair is the fallback if South Dakota text retrieval proves difficult.




3. Source documents required
For each case, four documents:

1. The initiative as certified for the ballot — the voter-enacted text. This is the base witness and the source of all spine IDs.
2. The amending or repealing bill as enacted — HB 567; HB 1069.
3. The current statutory sections, as they now stand in the state code.
4. Description materials for Layer 5 — bill title, official summary, fiscal note, sponsor statement, governor’s message where available.

Sources: state legislature websites, state statute compilations, secretary of state ballot measure archives. Ballotpedia measure pages are
acceptable for locating documents and for Layer 7 context fields, but primary text must come from primary sources.

Retrieval is the first task and the first gate. If any of the four documents cannot be obtained for a case within a reasonable effort,
that is a finding about the project’s feasibility and should be recorded rather than worked around silently.
4. Sequence
The order matters and should not be rearranged for convenience.


4.1 Hand-code first, with no pipeline
Both cases are coded entirely by hand before any code is written. Read the initiative, decompose it into provisions, assign spine IDs, read
the amending bill, and fill every layer manually.

This is not a validation step performed after the fact. It is the design input. Two things emerge only from reading actual statutory text:

    Categories that do not fit. A provision that is half obligation and half definition; a bill section that amends three provisions at once.
    The reach for a tag that does not exist. Noticing that you want to mark something the schema has no slot for is the most reliable
    signal that a layer is incomplete, and it does not happen while designing in the abstract.

Record time spent. Per case, and broken down by stage. This number sets project scope.

Record every schema friction encountered, even minor ones, in a running log. That log is an output of the prototype, not a
byproduct.


4.2 Then build the pipeline
Implement, in order:

1. Document retrieval and parsing into provisions
2. Spine ID assignment on the base witness
3. Embedding and hybrid retrieval (dense + BM25, reciprocal rank fusion)
4. Call 1 — alignment adjudication, including the none branch
5. Call 2 — effect classification, Layers 1 and 3, with rationale and source span
6. Call 3 — exemption sweep over the full amended statute
7. Layers 4 through 9 population
8. Output as structured JSON, one record per provision

Caching is implemented from the start, not retrofitted. The pipeline will be re-run many times as the codebook changes.


4.3 Then compare
Machine output against hand coding, per layer. Not a single accuracy number — a per-layer agreement figure, with every disagreement
inspected individually.

Every disagreement is read, not just counted. The distribution of disagreement types is more informative than the rate, and it tells
you whether the problem is the model, the prompt, or the codebook.




5. What the prototype produces
Six artifacts. Each is a deliverable, not a side effect.

1. Two hand-coded case files, complete across all nine layers.
2. Two machine-coded case files in the same schema.
3. A per-layer agreement report, with every disagreement inspected and categorized.
4. A schema friction log — categories that did not fit, values never used, values that were needed and absent.
5. A timing record — hours per case, by stage.
6. A working pipeline that runs on both cases from source documents to structured output.

No visualization. No website. Explicitly out of scope for this stage.

Criteria 4 and 5 are the two real tests. Criterion 4 tests whether the aligner can decline to match — the failure mode that silently
corrupts everything downstream. Criterion 5 tests whether the exemption sweep, the project’s most distinctive technical claim, does
what it claims.




7. Known risks
Alignment may not work on legal text. The central technical risk. Mitigation: hybrid retrieval rather than embeddings alone; the
none branch; hand-coding first so there is ground truth to measure against. If alignment fails, the project needs restructuring, and week
two is the right time to learn that.

The degenerate case may not be informative. If IM 22’s repeal is a single bill section repealing an entire chapter, there may be little
for the pipeline to do. Mitigation: this is still a valid negative-control test, and if it proves too thin, substitute Missouri Proposition B per
§2.5.

Document retrieval may be harder than expected, particularly the 2016 South Dakota text and the statutory sections as they stood
before repeal. Mitigation: retrieval is gate one; failure is recorded, not worked around.

Hand-coding may take far longer than estimated. This is not a risk to mitigate but a number to measure. If a case takes three days,
fifteen cases is not a semester and the scope decision changes.

The schema may prove substantially wrong. The most likely outcome, and the reason the prototype exists. Revisions are expected
and should be logged rather than silently applied.




8. Explicitly out of scope
     Any visualization, website, or interface work
     Cases beyond these two
     Cross-model agreement checking (deferred to the full build)
     Synthetic perturbation testing (deferred; hand-coding is the ground truth at this stage)
     The full corpus verification audit
     Any claim about what the findings mean




9. Open questions
Unresolved, and offered as such rather than papered over.

What is a provision? The unit of decomposition is not obvious. Statutory subsections do not map cleanly onto discrete obligations —
one subsection may carry three, and one obligation may span two. The hand-coding pass should produce a working rule, and that rule
should be written down.

How are bill sections that amend multiple provisions handled? One section of HB 567 may touch several spine IDs. The mapping is
many-to-many and the data model needs to accommodate it.
What is the base witness when the initiative was amended before it ever took effect? Not an issue for these two cases, but it will
arise.

How is none distinguished from retrieval failure? A provision with no counterpart and a provision whose counterpart the retriever
failed to surface look identical in the output. These need to be distinguishable or the deletion count is unreliable.

Does the exemption sweep need to run over the whole state code, or only the amended statute? Definitional narrowing can
occur in a general definitions chapter outside the statute being amended. The full-code version is more correct and much more
expensive. The prototype should scope to the amended statute and record whether that appears to have missed anything.

Is fifteen cases the right number? Deliberately unanswered. The timing record answers it.




10. What would make this prototype fail
Worth stating plainly, since it is easy to produce something that looks finished and taught nothing:

    Hand-coding done after seeing machine output, or skipped
    Disagreements counted but not read
    An empty friction log
    The exemption sweep implemented but never tested against a case where it should fire
    Timing not recorded
    Schema revisions applied silently rather than logged
