## 290.502 — Minimum wage rate — increase or decrease, when. (CURRENT, in force)

290.502. 1. Except as may be otherwise provided pursuant to sections 290.500 to 290.530, effective January 1, 2007, every employer shall pay to each employee wages at the rate of $6.50 per hour, or wages at the same rate or rates set under the provisions of federal law as the prevailing federal minimum wage applicable to those covered jobs in interstate commerce, whichever rate per hour is higher.

2. The minimum wage shall be increased or decreased on January 1, 2008, and on January 1 of successive years but ending December 31, 2024, by the increase or decrease in the cost of living. On September 30, 2007, and on each September 30 of each successive year but ending December 31, 2024, the director shall measure the increase or decrease in the cost of living by the percentage increase or decrease as of the preceding July over the level as of July of the immediately preceding year of the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W) or successor index as published by the U.S. Department of Labor or its successor agency, with the amount of the minimum wage increase or decrease rounded to the nearest five cents.

3. Except as may be otherwise provided pursuant to sections 290.500 to 290.530, and notwithstanding subsection 1 of this section, effective January 1, 2025, every employer shall pay to each employee wages at the rate of not less than $13.75 per hour, or wages at the same rate or rates set under the provisions of federal law as the federal minimum wage applicable to those covered jobs in interstate commerce, whichever rate per hour is higher. Thereafter, the minimum wage established by this subsection shall be increased by $1.25 per hour, to $15.00 per hour, effective January 1, 2026. If at any time the federal minimum wage rate is above or is thereafter increased above the minimum wage then in effect under this subsection, the higher federal rate shall immediately become the minimum wage required by this subsection.

4. For purposes of this section, the term "public employer" means an employer that is the state or a political subdivision of the state, including a department, agency, officer, bureau, division, board, commission, or instrumentality of the state, or a city, county, town, village, school district, or other political subdivision of the state. Beginning on August 28, 2025, the provisions of this section shall apply to a public employer with respect to its employees.

**Effective:** August 28, 2025

**Legislative history (source note, revisor.mo.gov):** L. 1990 H.B. 1881 § 2; A.L. 2006 Amended by Initiative Proposition B (November 7, 2006); A.L. 2018 Amended by Initiative Proposition B (November 6, 2018); A.L. 2024 Amended by Initiative Proposition A (November 5, 2024); A.L. 2025 H.B. 567, et al.

Comparison note: the only substantive change from the Proposition A-enacted text is (a) deletion of the post-2026 CPI-indexed annual adjustment (formerly subsection 3's final clause), and (b) the flip of subsection 4 from exempting public employers to including them, effective August 28, 2025. See `02-HB567-enacted.md` for the bracket/bold markup showing exactly what changed.

## 290.600 through 290.642 — Earned Paid Sick Time program (REPEALED, no longer in force)

290.600. **(Repealed L. 2025 H.B. 567, et al.)**

The Revisor's page for section 290.600 (and, per the same repealing act, sections 290.603, 290.606, 290.609, 290.612, 290.615, 290.618, 290.621, 290.624, 290.627, 290.630, 290.633, 290.636, 290.639, and 290.642) now displays only a repeal notation — no operative statutory text remains. The Revisor's site shows "2 histories" for section 290.600: the version enacted by Proposition A was operative November 5, 2024 through August 28, 2025, at which point the repeal effected by HB 567 took effect. There is no successor/replacement section; Missouri's Revised Statutes currently contain no state-law paid-sick-leave mandate for private employers in Chapter 290.

Current status confirmed directly from revisor.mo.gov (queried 2026-09-27): section 290.600 page text reads "290.600. (Repealed L. 2025 H.B. 567, et al.)" with repeal effective August 28, 2025.

## Bottom line — what remains of Chapter 290's Proposition A provisions as of today (2026-09-27)

| Provision | Status |
|---|---|
| $13.75/hr (2025) minimum wage | In force (was already effective before HB 567) |
| $15.00/hr (2026) minimum wage | In force, unchanged by HB 567 |
| Post-2026 CPI indexing of minimum wage | **Repealed** by HB 567 |
| Public-employer exemption from state minimum wage | **Reversed** by HB 567 — public employers now covered |
| Earned paid sick time (accrual, use, notice, enforcement, private right of action) — §§290.600–290.642 | **Repealed in full** by HB 567, effective August 28, 2025 |

Sections 290.500–290.530 (the pre-existing minimum wage framework that 290.502 sits within) were not independently checked in this research pass; they were not touched by HB 567's repeal-and-reenact clause and are presumed unchanged. If needed for the capstone, they should be pulled separately from revisor.mo.gov.
