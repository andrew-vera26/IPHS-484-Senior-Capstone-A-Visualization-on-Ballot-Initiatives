## 1. IM 22 official title and summary

"South Dakota Government Accountability and Anti-Corruption Act" — an initiated state statute "to increase
accountability to the people of South Dakota in electoral politics by revising certain provisions concerning
campaign finance and lobbying, establishing an ethics commission, creating a democracy credit program, and
making an appropriation therefor." (Source: certified text, see `01-initiative-text.md`.)

## 2. Election results

November 8, 2016 general election: 180,634 votes (51.63%) in favor, 169,199 (48.37%) against.

## 3. Campaign funding

Support: "South Dakotans for Ethics Reform." Represent.Us (Northampton, MA-based national anti-corruption
group) contributed approximately $1,000,000, reported as 57% of the campaign's total funding — implying total
support-side funding on the order of $1.75 million. (Source: Ballotpedia campaign-finance summary.)

Opposition: "Defeat 22," including Americans for Prosperity–South Dakota, which specifically opposed the
public-financing ("democracy credit") provisions as a use of taxpayer dollars to fund campaigns. No aggregate

## 4. HB 1069 (2017) legislative history

- Bill: HB 1069, "An Act to repeal and revise certain provisions related to campaign finance and to declare an
  emergency." Enacted as 2017 SL ch. 72.
- Introduced: January 20, 2017.
- House State Affairs Committee: heard January 23, 2017.
- House passage: 54-13, January 24, 2017.
- Senate State Affairs Committee: heard January 25, 2017.
- Senate passage: 27-8, February 1, 2017 (Senate amendment on Jan. 26, 2017 removed certain overstrikes from
  the House-engrossed bill per the Senate Journal — the substance of that floor amendment was not individually
  re-verified against the final enrolled text this pass).
- Signed by Governor Dennis Daugaard: February 2, 2017.
- Emergency clause: yes — "this Act is necessary for the support of the state government and its existing
  public institutions, an emergency is hereby declared to exist" — making the repeal effective immediately
  upon signature and, under the South Dakota Constitution, not subject to a people's-veto referendum. This is
  the case's defining procedural fact and the reason it was selected as a contrast to Prop A's ordinary
  (non-emergency) amendment.
- Dissent: Senators Billie Sutton, Troy Heinert, and Jason Frerichs filed a formal dissent/protest arguing a
  pecuniary-interest conflict — legislators who were plaintiffs in the pending suit against IM 22 (see below)
  stood to avoid further litigation costs if the repeal passed. (Source: WebSearch summary of the Senate
  Journal; the dissent's exact journal citation was not independently pulled this pass.)
- (Sources: SD LRC session-law/journal documents at mylrc.sdlegislature.gov, cross-checked against Ballotpedia
  and contemporaneous press coverage — KELOLAND, IVN, Missouri Lawyers Media-style wire coverage, SD Searchlight.)

## 5. Litigation

Twenty-five Republican state legislators and other individuals filed suit in the South Dakota Sixth Judicial
Circuit on November 23, 2016, challenging IM 22's constitutionality. Circuit Court Judge Mark Barnett heard
arguments December 8, 2016, and granted a preliminary injunction finding IM 22 likely unconstitutional "beyond
a reasonable doubt," holding that the measure's potentially unconstitutional portions could not be severed
from the rest — the Act would stand or fall as a whole. HB 1069's repeal (effective immediately via emergency
clause, Feb. 2, 2017) mooted the case before any final merits ruling was issued. (Source: WebSearch summary
citing contemporaneous coverage; the circuit court's written injunction order itself was not independently
retrieved this pass — gap noted explicitly.)

## 6. Subsequent similar measure

South Dakota Constitutional Amendment W (2018) — "State Campaign Finance and Lobbying Laws, Government
Accountability Board, and Initiative Process Amendment" — an initiated constitutional amendment that would
have re-enacted similar reforms (as a constitutional amendment, specifically to make them harder for the
legislature to repeal by ordinary statute, as happened to IM 22). **Defeated** at the November 6, 2018 general
election: 142,769 (45.06%) yes to 174,081 (54.94%) no. (Source: Ballotpedia.)
