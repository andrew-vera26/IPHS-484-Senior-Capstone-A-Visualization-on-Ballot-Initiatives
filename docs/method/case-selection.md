# Case selection

## Design

The cases are chosen for diversity, not sampled. With a small, non-random set, a claim of representativeness would be false. The claim is instead that the cases span the dimensions along which alteration varies, and the criteria and exclusions are published so a reader can see what was left out.

## Tiers

- **Tier 1.** Every eligible case is machine-coded by the pipeline.
- **Tier 2.** About fifteen cases are hand-verified, and these carry the validation claims.

## Dimensions to span

- Rule regime: unrestricted, restricted, purpose-fidelity
- Policy domain
- Speed and form of alteration (immediate repeal, gradual amendment, adopt-and-amend)
- Outcome (largely deleted, partly narrowed, largely preserved)

## Mandatory counter-cases

Technical corrections, implementation bills, and unaltered controls. Without them, every case is one where something went wrong, and the method cannot show it distinguishes a hollowing from a fix.

## Candidates

**All unverified.** Each must be confirmed against primary sources before use.

| Case | Why it is a candidate |
|---|---|
| Missouri Prop A (2024) | Prototype case. Near-total deletion by HB 567, with one provision partially surviving. Layer 4 is almost untested here |
| Missouri Prop B (2024) | Intended second case, so Layer 4 gets variation |
| South Dakota IM 22 (2016) | Deletions and replacement, the benchmark for the `none` branch |
| Michigan paid sick leave (2018) | Adopt-and-amend, and a later judicial restoration |
| Maine or another case | A third regime |

## Exclusions

Constitutional amendments, local measures, and measures from before the chosen window. Excluded cases are listed on this page when decisions are made, with the reason.
